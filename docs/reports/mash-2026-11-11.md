# Mash report 2026-11-11: is one-button play enough?

Status: measurement, draft for the Lead and the owner. No rules constant, content value, balance target or spec was changed; src/ untouched.

Question [stated: docs/feedback/2026-11-11-1841-owner.md]: the owner played Hunter runs by pressing key 1 over and over, without picking targets, and got through. Is one-button play enough, for every class, with the talents of that attack? Follow-up 18:46 [stated]: mashing a rotation on normal mobs is fine (classic grind); rares, elites, dungeon bosses and new enemy types must make the player stop and think, and that should happen every so often.

- Engine and simulator: main 3e16499 (engine M2b v12 as implemented in c0ab802, the onebutton bot and the think replays); the rotation replays (`--think-mash`) at aa33d22, same engine and bots (the onebutton runs are identical run for run to 3e16499's: checked on all 3 000 classic P0 runs).
- Script: `bash sim/measure-mash.sh sim/out/mash` (stages A-D), rotation replays with `--think-mash`; tables `node sim/mashreport.mjs --in sim/out/mash`.
- Seeds 1000 onward, paired by class and seed across bots. Starting tables: classic, the 11-11b planner warm-up tables; expedition, the 11-11f node-risk observations with the v11 table pass's `ct` and `dim` (as 11-11g).
- Rates: Wilson 95 %; paired differences: normal interval of the paired difference.

## Summary for the owner

Answer on the classic map, P0 (fresh profile; 1 000 runs per class; PALL and the expedition follow below as they finish):

1. **One-button play is not enough to win.** Pressing only key 1, with the talents of that attack and a potion under 35 % HP, beat the end boss once in 3 000 runs (autoplay 33.3 %, planned openings 34.0 %), and reached the boss in 46.9 % of runs against 82.6 % for autoplay (-35.7 pp, paired).
   - **Rogue: no.** Gutting Jab alone builds combo points it never spends: 24 % reach the boss (autoplay 81 %), 0 wins.
   - **Mage: closest.** Ember Bolt is the Mage's real damage: 60 % reach the boss (autoplay 79 %), 0 wins.
   - **Hunter (the owner's case): levelling partly, the end boss no.** 56 % reach the boss (autoplay 88 %), 0 wins.
   - Part of the gap is the tree, not the button: autoplay on the slot-1 trees reaches the boss 70.2 % (wins 25.0 %), so one button costs 23 pp of reach against the same character.
2. **The grind is easy: fine, as the owner wants.** On the same fights (paired replays), one-button play dies in 1.3 % of normal quest fights, 0.3 % of grind waves and 0.4 % of dungeon trash fights, and loses about as much HP as autoplay (normal fight: 19.5 against 19.7 % of max HP). A planned opening saves about 7 % of max HP per normal fight, not lives.
3. **The special fights are hard for one-button play: wanted, and it holds.** On the same fights, one-button play dies in 12.6 % of elite fights (opener line 1.1 %), 32.9 % of dungeon-boss fights (1.4 %) and 42.3 % of rare fights (19.7 %). The opener line survives 84 % of the fights one-button play dies in, and 71 % of those are special fights. One-button play answers 4.8 % of the wind-ups in elite, rare and dungeon-boss fights; autoplay 73 %, the opener 78 %.
4. **How often the player must think.** For one-button play a think fight comes every 4.2 nodes (6.8 per run), but four in five are not special fights: mostly normal and grind fights in which the planned opening saves 20 % of max HP or more, not a life. For a player who repeats a damage rotation (the masher on the same fights) a think fight comes every 6.1 nodes; special fights are think fights 58 % of the time for it, normal and grind fights 10 %.
5. **Where the game misses the owner's target today.**
   - Zone 1 teaches nothing: think fights are 7 % of Z1 fights and its special fights only 22 % (Z2-Z5: 63-86 %).
   - Something new is not harder than something known: normal and grind fights with an enemy met for the first time in the run are think fights 16.0 % of the time, familiar ones 16.3 %.
   - Potions carry one-button play: without them it reaches the boss in 31.3 % of runs (with them 46.9 %).
   - The end boss is the hard skill check: one-button play never wins it, the masher 1.4 % of the runs that reach it, autoplay 40 %.

**Think fight** [assumption: "clearly more HP", Lead's message 18:46]: every non-boss fight of a onebutton run is replayed from the same state and fight stream with the opener's opening then autoplay (the "opener line") and with autoplay. A think fight is one in which onebutton dies (or needs a resurrect) where the opener line does not, or ends it at least 20 % of max HP lower (SIM.THINK_HP_GAP; 10 and 30 % shown too). A **rotation think fight** is the same test for the masher's replay of that fight: the fight still punishes a player who repeats a fixed damage rotation.

## Where one-button play fails and where it passes

Classic P0, same fights (300 onebutton runs; the tables below list every archetype and the enemies with at least 25 replayed fights):

- **Fails: dungeon bosses and elites with a wind-up, rares.** The worst enemies are dungeon bosses: Ammeset, Voice of Veth (caster; think fight 94 %, one-button play dies 57 %), Father Vael the Unfaithful (caster; 94 %, 40 %), Mawgrim of the Locks (brute; 68 %, 32 %); then elites: Mawra Ironbrow (guardian; 79 %, 21 %), Kharun the Unburied (brute; 73 %). Their signature wind-ups land when nobody answers them, and a landed wind-up empowers the enemy for the rest of the fight.
- **Fails: casters and brutes.** Fights with only casters: one-button play dies 15.9 % (opener line 0.2 %); only brutes: 9.6 % (1.4 %). Their turn-2 Hex Bolt and Heavy Smash are what a fixed button cannot answer.
- **Passes easily: beasts, skulkers, menders and guardians alone, treasure guards, most dungeon trash.** Beast-only fights: think 3.8 %, one-button play dies 1.4 %; skulker-only 4.7 %, 0.8 %; Peat Sentinel and Stonewall Sentinel (guardians, normal fights) 3-4 %; Cellar Rat Swarm and Rust-Shackled Guard (trash) 0 %.
- **Normal fights cost HP, not runs.** Even the worst normal enemy (Slag Brute, a brute in normal and grind fights) is a think fight half the time but kills one-button play in 0.8 % of fights. HP lost in normal fights is refilled by regen and food between fights [inference: from the death rates], so the grind rarely forces a decision.

## Readings and assumptions
1. The default target is the engine's rules 7.9 choice (mender, then caster, then lowest HP), which is what an action aims at when the player picks no target. "Not choosing a target" therefore already hits the healer or caster first; this is part of why normal fights are easy.
2. HP lost per fight is the HP at the start minus the HP at the end, before post-fight regen, in % of max HP; potions drunk in the fight count as HP not lost. A death counts as losing all HP left.
3. The opener plans the first 5 rounds only (OPEN_ROUNDS), then plays autoplay; its full runs are expensive (about 2.3 s per run), so they ran 100 runs per class on classic P0 and fewer elsewhere (sample sizes in the tables). The think replays give the opener's line on the same fights as onebutton, which is the paired comparison.
4. The think sample is the first 100 seeds per class (50 for PALL and the expedition), whose onebutton runs are identical to the first runs of the full onebutton cell; for the rogue these 100 seeds are a harder sample (12 % reach the boss, 24 % in all 1 000).
5. Rotation replays (masher on the same fights) cover all 1 000 onebutton runs per class on classic P0 and PALL, and the 50 think seeds per class on the expedition.
6. The record field fc at 3e16499 wrote '?' for dungeon trash and shardspawn enemies; fight kinds come from the fight itself, so no table is affected (fixed in aa33d22).
