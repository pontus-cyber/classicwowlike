# Owner feedback 2026-11-11 18:41

> I noticed when I was playing Hunter for a full run or a couple of runs, I could mash the number one attack over and over and over again without paying too much attention on choosing the specific target. I think you should try to run this in the simulation, and maybe with other characters as well, and also choose to go by the talents of that specific attack as well, and then try to do a couple of hundreds or thousands of runs in that way. If it's too easy to play the game by just pressing the same button over and over again without thinking, then it's not stimulating enough, it's not challenging enough, so we need to work with that. Try to do some research and try to do some runs and see what you get on your tests.

## Lead's plan
- Sim: a one-button bot (slot 1 only, default target, potion at low HP, no other decisions in fight), talents into the slot-1 attack's tree, all three classes, P0 and PALL, classic and expedition with start camp, 1 000+ runs per class per cell. Compare with planner/opener: reach the boss, deaths per zone, HP lost per fight, rounds per fight, boss win.
- Existing data: the masher bot (biggest-damage button, ignores intents) reaches the boss about as often as autoplay (11-10d: 78.7 % vs 79.8 %); B49 asks for a gap. So mashing being enough in levelling fights is a known open issue; this measures the stricter one-button case.
- Then design: levers that make one-button play fail in levelling fights without punishing planned play (research: how other turn-based games punish repetition: enemy intents that punish a fixed answer, resource or cooldown on the filler, target choice that matters).
