## Lead's plan
- Sim: a one-button bot (slot 1 only, default target, potion at low HP, no other decisions in fight), talents into the slot-1 attack's tree, all three classes, P0 and PALL, classic and expedition with start camp, 1 000+ runs per class per cell. Compare with planner/opener: reach the boss, deaths per zone, HP lost per fight, rounds per fight, boss win.
- Existing data: the masher bot (biggest-damage button, ignores intents) reaches the boss about as often as autoplay (11-10d: 78.7 % vs 79.8 %); B49 asks for a gap. So mashing being enough in levelling fights is a known open issue; this measures the stricter one-button case.
- Then design: levers that make one-button play fail in levelling fights without punishing planned play (research: how other turn-based games punish repetition: enemy intents that punish a fixed answer, resource or cooldown on the filler, target choice that matters).

## Follow-up 18:46 (owner)
> What I like with Slay the Spire is that when you enter the game, you have to be extremely cautious with your every move that you take. And that might be because there is a limitation on how much you can do each run, and you can also stack your attacks. So that's add to the complexity. But we don't want to really copy that right off, but we want to get the same feel and effect of weighing your choices going into the combat. But then I'm also fine with casual mobs being simple grind, just as it is in Classic. You do your same attack rotation when you identify a mob that you already recognize and you know what this mob is gonna do. And that's fine. That's what the grinding are for. We're meeting regular mobs and doing the same rotation over and over again just to grind. That's the good feel of it. But then the game throws in this rare mob or something new, and then you need to stop and think. And that's when the challenge comes. And that should happen ever so often, so it keeps you on your toes.

## Design target from this (Lead, draft)
- Normal mobs: a known rotation is fine; mashing may work there. Not a problem to fix.
- Rares, elites, dungeon bosses and new enemy types: one-button play must fail often enough that the player stops and thinks; the right answer depends on what that enemy does.
- Frequency: a "think" fight should come up regularly (e.g. every few nodes), not only at the end boss.
- Resources limited per run (potions, cooldowns, Focus/mana across fights) give weight to each choice, without copying a deck system.
